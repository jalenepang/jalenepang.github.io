USER RESEARCH

UI/UX

Adobook is a digital recipe book designed as an interactive mobile app allowing users to easily store, organize, and access their personal recipes. Created with accessibility in mind, it supports features like image scanning to convert handwritten or printed recipes into text, reducing the need for manual input. The app focuses on simplicity and inclusivity, making it especially suitable for users of all ages, including seniors.

back

Adobook hopes to keep Filipino traditions alive and preserve culture through food and community.

research

The purpose of this project was to create an interactive system. The team's first step included determining a target audience which we eventually narrowed down to a specific domain of people in which we could make a significant impact. This involved understanding their objectives, routines, potential growth areas, and obstacles. By focusing our scope on imigrant Filipino familes and interviewing them, I crafted one of three personas to guide the subsequent brainstorming sessions effectively.

domain & persona

Given the app's ethos, which emphasizes fostering a sense of family and serving as a portal for passing down recipes through generations, it was crucial to maintain a strong focus on Family. To achieve this, I developed a persona named Ashley Garcia, representing one of our primary audience segments. Ashley's persona details her relationship with her grandma and her characteristics, including pain points, goals, and motivations, ensuring our app resonates deeply with users like her.

ideation

During the brainstorming phase, we explored three potential directions for the project: a Recipe Translator, an Online Recipe-Sharing System, and a Digital Recipe Book. Following a constructive critique session, it was decided that the Digital Recipe Book held the most promise and aligned best with our goals.

Like most design processes, I began by sketching out various concepts to generate ideas and visualize possibilities. Although each team member contributed their own designs, the final concept closely resembled my initial sketches. I subsequently transitioned these ideas into an initial Figma design, where we collaborated as a team to integrate everyone's input and refine the design.

A style guide was created to give each team member the freedom to design while working within the predetermined brand guidelines. This provided creative flexibility while ensuring that everyone’s work remained cohesive and consistent with the overall visual identity.

design

As we moved into the design phase, all team members collaborated to identify and develop the essential features of Adobook. With our end-users in mind, the team selected several key features, including viewing recipes, scanning or manually entering recipes, and translating recipes into different languages. Other aspects we also considered were the app’s layout and usability, focusing on a simple design with large, accessible buttons to make navigation easier for seniors.

challenges

UNFOCUSED TARGET AUDIENCE

While the overall progress remained steady, there were hurdles that highlighted the necessity of aligning Adobook more closely with its intended users. The app was developed with a focus on features like recipe addition and data collection, however, our initial user flow involved users manually inserting data through a fill-in-the-blank form. This led to an apparent challenge we faced during a class critique, where feedback indicated that this manual approach to data entry did not serve the app's target users well. Given that it's demographic includes seniors who may rely solely on hard copy recipes, Adobook needed to offer a more user-friendly solution for recipe entry.

AUTOMATIC SCAN FUNCTION

To address this challenge, I proposed a feature enabling image scanning. This function would convert images into text, alleviating the need for manual data entry and thereby easing the usability for our target audience. Additionally, I proposed that the app should support audio recordings to further improved accessibility.

pilot testing

When the designs were completed, we conducted a pilot usability test with 9 participants (6 in-person & 3 online) to further refine our design and enhance the app's user interface experience. Including a Filipino family of three, the participants who were gathered for this usability test belonged to our user domain.

Participants were tasked with completing a series of interactions correlating to the main features of our prototype. After completing these tasks, users were asked to reflect on and answer questions relating to their experience. I assumed the role of observer and note-taker in this test, documenting events and feedback.

Below is a summarized overview of the test results, along with the issues I pinpointed during the session. While participants were very particular in their assessment of the interface design, this experience brought Adobook to a higher level of fidelity. I found the session highly informative, shedding light on users' perspectives and insights throughout the process.

adjustments

Simplified the dashboard, removing the redundant "All Recipes" button and integrating tag creation into the process of uploading a receipe.

Ensured there is always an emergency exit to prevent potential errors and close out of a flow without negative consequences.

Implemented recipe sharing via external communication apps and introduced an accessible button for quickly printing physical copies.

conclusion

Adobook turned out to be a well-researched project designed with its target users in mind. Researching a specific demographic helps guide a project in the right direction by providing a clearer understanding of the needs, preferences, and expectations of its intended audience. This ensures that the app is designed to address the needs of real users rather than relying solely on the assumptions of its designers. I learned the importance of conducting user testing, as feedback from intended users can reveal valuable insights and potential improvements that designers might otherwise overlook.

Through this project, I gained experience in task allocation and consistent scheduling, including regular check-ins with team members. By holding each other accountable and sharing constructive feedback, we were able to make steady progress while tools like a style guide also helped us maintain consistency throughout our work.
