RESEARCH

Bandsintown Amplify is a live music booking and networking platform for artists and venues. By streamlining the processes required to create a live show, Bandsintown Amplify provides artists and venues an efficient and direct communication channel when booking that the existing Bandsintown does not. Large portions of reading and research were done beforehand to justify how and why this design helps a particular pain point amongst specific intended users in the local music community.

research

Phase one of this project was heavily focused on reading about experience design. We learned through the lens of the intersection of business and design, examining how design operates within business. The reading touches on concepts such as a holistic experience whereby every touchpoint should be considered, through an iterative approach, to extend value to customers over time across all of the business's activities

Experience Design is, as the title of the Newbery and Farnham book suggests, a strategy and framework for integrating brand, experience and value. Using the customer journey diagram, we mapped the different stages of interaction for Philz Coffee and IKEA, looking into how each touchpoint contributes to the overall experience. This allowed us to practice identifying areas where value could be lost over time, as well as opportunities to strengthen engagement and re-engage customers beyond their initial interaction.

potential clients & problems

The next step included communicating this new learning and understanding of experience design through case studies that we selected as a team. We focused on analyzing the case studies to further decipher how brand value pillars should be implemented at each touchpoint to provide customers the long-term value during and after interaction. For each client, we found insights on potential problem spaces, evidence to prove the problem is real, and framing the issue to express "how might we…", "in order to…".

GMK
GMK is a premium keycap manufacturer whose products are primarily discovered and purchased through third-party vendors and enthusiast communities. Research revealed that newcomers struggle to find reliable information on releases, purchasing options, and production updates, creating a high barrier to entry and contributing to counterfeit purchases. This led to the question: How might we connect newcomers and beginners to the necessary informational touchpoints needed to research, purchase, and stay updated on GMK keycaps in order to connect GMK directly with their customers, identify available purchase options, and provide production updates to reduce the high barrier of entry to GMK keycap designs?

THE ODD BUNCH
The Odd Bunch reduces food waste by selling surplus produce at lower prices. While this creates an affordable option for families facing rising grocery costs, research showed that meal planning and dietary flexibility remain challenges, particularly for households with limited time and picky eaters. This led to the question: How might we enable and support financially struggling families to incorporate diverse seasonal produce in order to promote healthy eating, making cooking and shopping efficient, while reducing food waste?

PEERSPACE
Peerspace helps users book unique venues for events, but event planning extends far beyond securing a space. Research found that planners must independently source vendors, entertainment, and other services, while hosts are often expected to fill these gaps. This led to the question: How might we provide event planners holistic support during the event planning process (conception to execution) without burdening the venue hosts in order to efficiently craft a memorable and smooth event, leaving both event planners and hosts content with Peerspace?

Our final project ultimately shifted from the topics discussed above. As a group, we decided to focus on Bandsintown, exploring its online experience as a live music discovery platform. The following sections detail this direction and how we narrowed our focus.

why bandsintown?

Currently, the Bandsintown platform focuses on promotions catering mainly to artist-to-fan interactions, with less emphasis on the end process occurring before a show - how it is organized and scheduled. It loses points in terms of what goes into creating a bill and how that touchpoint is difficult for local artists and small venues who may not have the connections necessary.

As opposed to Bandsintown's brand pillars, Songkick, a competitor service, prioritizes fan engagement by "always putting fans first." Functionality is also gated towards larger artists, with specific features lacked until an artist has a large enough following.

OPPORTUNITY TO INTERVENE

Pre-show and post-show touchpoints are equally—if not more—important for small artists and venues in organizing a successful live show, yet its never fully addressed on the platform.

FRAMING

how might we strengthen and streamline the connection and concert creation processes for local artists and small venues in order to reinforce the local music scene’s ability to facilitate creative opportunities?

target audience

Local artists have to handle their own booking and promotion. A lack of connection to booking processes makes it difficult to connect with venues and other artists. Currently, Bandsintown artists reach out to other artists to build a bill primarily through Instagram and other social media platforms. Only after accommodating all participating musicians' schedules can they email a chosen venue to request to perform. Getting involved in the local music scene by performing at local venues allows them to connect with peers and generate more creative opportunities.

The role of the booking agent is to discover, review, and book artists to perform at their venue. Small venues, on the other hand, lack the resources of larger venues, including a dedicated booking agent.

These small venues accommodate local music scenes, serving as important spaces for artists to advance their careers. Their value as social hubs and niche spaces of cultural production become an important ‘stepping stone’ for building musicians’ careers.

VALUE PROPOSITION:

to bandsintown

Foster and boost engagement among current members while attracting more musicians and venues to Bandsintown.

Opportunity to showcase a broader, more diverse lineup of concerts while bringing visibility to local events for its audience.

to local artists

Optimized organization of concerts removes the stress of planning, allowing artists to focus more on preparing their music. Eliminating the hassle of crafting perfect emails for every pitch by managing all outreach and proposals directly through the platform means more efficient and direct communication.

to small venues

Providing a streamlined booking process can foster and boost

engagement among current members while attracting more musicians and venues to Bandsintown. By catering to smaller artist and venues, Bandsintown can showcase a broader, more diverse lineup of concerts while bringing visibility to local events.

OUR INTERVENTION

artist enagement

Discover, connect, and stay up to date with others in your local music scene. 'Tune in' to fellow artists and venues to keep track of upcoming shows, see who's attending, and create more opportunities to connect and collaborate.

view requests

Venues make informed booking decisions by viewing artist booking requests. All necessary information is aggregated in one place, including music samples, performance videos, and scheduling information.

booking

A guided, step-by-step booking process designed for clarity and accessibility. Artists can discover and invite collaborators, while clear button states distinguish pending invitations from accepted requests as they build their bill.

challenges

FRAMING, INITIAL DESIGN SOLUTION, & MOVING TO MOBILE

Our initial design solution focused on gamifying the experience by allowing artists and venues to “match” with one another based on their needs and musical styles. This would make it easier for artists to discover suitable venues and for venues to find artists who fit their space. However, after several rounds of class critiques, we realized that this approach did not truly address the problem of finding and connecting with venues. Instead, it functioned more like a curated list of suggested artists and venues, with the “matching” mechanic adding novelty without meaningfully improving the process.

We ultimately pivoted toward a “Tune In” function that more closely follows an interaction model users are already familiar with: social media. Rather than creating an entirely new matching system, the feature would allow artists, venues, and audiences to discover and follow one another while keeping relevant information curated within a single platform. As Bandsintown is already an established platform, this intervention could also integrate naturally into its existing ecosystem rather than functioning as a separate service. We further structured the experience into clear steps with calendar integration, making it more than simply a messaging tool as our initial concept would have been. Although the idea of matching artists and venues based on musical styles and preferences was interesting, we ultimately felt that our final approach was better aligned with our stated problem and provided a more effective solution.

Another challenge we ran into centred on how we presented and communicated the design problem. We struggled to convince our audience that the problem we had identified was significant enough to warrant intervention. In early weeks, feedback made it clear that our proposed solution lacked excitement, while critics were also unconvinced that the problem itself was an issue that genuinely needed to be addressed.

However, during office hours, we received feedback that our actual reasoning was clear when explained conversationally. This made us realize that the issue was not necessarily with our justification, but with how effectively we communicated it through our presentation. To address this, we reframed our project to more clearly communicate the *why* behind our intervention and how it could benefit Bandsintown as a business while also supporting local artists and small venues. Given the nature of our proposed features, we were further encouraged to consider a mobile platform, which would provide users with more portable and accessible communication while aligning with a platform they are already accustomed to using.

conclusion

This project pushed me to develop a deeper understanding of experience design through both research and application. Its structured process of constant, weekly presentations and critiques encouraged me to continuously iterate and defend design decisions. Rather than simply presenting a solution, I learned not only the importance of building a strong rationale behind each design choice but also communicating and justifying those ideas clearly to an audience.

As a team project with a substantial workload, I learned to divide responsibilities effectively while maintaining a shared direction. Working independently and then reconvening to share research, readings, and design work required us to organize our thoughts, identify the most valuable insights, and then determine what should inform our next steps. Because the research phases played such a major role in shaping our project, it taught me that good design is not just about developing a solution, but also about understanding where a potential design lives and why it is needed.
